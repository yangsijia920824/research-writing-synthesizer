# Provenance and Quality Control

## Maintain a Claim Ledger

For each important drafted claim, retain:

```text
Draft claim:
Writing purpose:
Source ID(s):
Exact supporting location:
Evidence type:
Direct or secondary citation:
Verification status:
Expression examples used:
Notes or uncertainty:
```

Expression examples and factual evidence may come from different sources. Keep them distinct.

## Verify Citations

- Open the cited source and confirm it supports the exact claim.
- Check sample, model, conditions, endpoints, and scope.
- Trace secondary citations to the underlying source when feasible.
- Do not inherit a citation solely because another paper placed it after a similar sentence.
- Preserve uncertainty, disagreement, and boundary conditions.
- Mark claims needing verification rather than filling gaps from memory.

## Corpus Integrity

Confirm:

- all selected papers were imported;
- target sections are complete;
- every labeled unit remains in the complete map;
- moving units did not create omissions;
- duplicated use is intentional and traceable;
- the residual queue was reviewed before completion;
- article and paragraph provenance remains recoverable.

## Outline Audit

- Every major move advances the core contribution.
- The sequence answers the reader's likely questions.
- The gap is supported by search evidence.
- The importance of the gap is explained.
- Prior solutions are represented fairly.
- The proposed approach follows from the stated limitations.
- Alternative interpretations are not hidden.
- The "this work" passage matches actual results and scope.

## Draft Audit

- Claims are evidence-backed.
- Citations are attached to the correct claims.
- Terminology is consistent.
- Causal language does not exceed the evidence.
- Adjacent sentences perform distinct functions.
- Transitions match real relations.
- Source-specific wording has been structurally rewritten.
- Repetition, ornamental background, and unsupported generalization are removed.
- The section length matches its role in the manuscript.

## Machine-Generated Text

Treat model-generated prose as a candidate, not as evidence. Check:

- invented citations;
- merged facts from incompatible studies;
- exaggerated consensus;
- reversed comparisons;
- missing qualifiers;
- polished but unsupported causal links;
- language copied too closely from supplied sources.

## Decision States

Use explicit statuses:

- **Ready:** argument, evidence, provenance, and wording pass review.
- **Revise:** structure or wording is weak but evidence is adequate.
- **Evidence needed:** a necessary claim lacks reliable support.
- **Reframe:** the selected evidence does not support the proposed narrative.
- **Hold:** study-specific claims depend on results not yet available.

Deliver unresolved issues alongside the draft so fluency is never mistaken for completion.
